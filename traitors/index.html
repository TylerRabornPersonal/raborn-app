Three questions nobody has actually answered.

01

How many Traitors are there? Nobody knows. Nobody has ever known.

The whole room has been reasoning off the number three. That number has no source. It was never announced. It came from one comment about how the TV show usually starts.

Kaylee asked outright on Jul 28: do we know how many Traitors there are? Kobi answered no. Yennhi confirmed MK had said no. Then Ella said the show usually starts with three — and the room adopted it as fact.

Ella also raised recruitment: when a Traitor is banished, a new one can be turned in secret. Five banishments later, nobody has asked whether that happened here.

Ask the host

MK — how many Traitors did this game start with, and has anyone been recruited since?

02

The teams are random. MK said so. So why is anyone using them as evidence?

She stated it in the rules for Wordle Wars on Jul 27: you have been randomly assigned to one of three teams. Take her at her word — she has been straight with us all game.

Now sit with what that means. If the teams are random, team membership carries no information whatsoever about who is a Traitor. None. It is a coin toss wearing a name tag. And yet almost every accusation for two weeks has started with which team somebody was on.

Team 3 lost the Wordle, so Team 3 must have a Traitor. Team 1 won it, so Team 1 is clean. Team 2 kept the shield, so Team 2 is guilty. All three of those arguments are reading meaning into a randomizer.
Ask the host

MK — were these teams randomly selected, as it was stated in the rules they would be?

03

“One Traitor per team” is a guess stacked on a guess.

Ryan's final theory was one Traitor per group, which narrowed Team 1 down to Ella or me. Random assignment does not hand you an even spread — that is the opposite of what random means. Three Traitors dropped at random into teams of 5, 6 and 6 land one apiece in only 180 of 680 possible arrangements.

And that is the best case, granting a Traitor count nobody has ever confirmed.

That is roughly 26%. Which means there is about a 74% chance the premise everyone is voting on is simply wrong.
