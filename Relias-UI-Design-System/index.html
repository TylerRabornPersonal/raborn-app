Design Principles

Six principles that guide every decision.

Before color, type, or component, these principles answer the question of why. Every layout choice, interaction pattern, and copy decision should be traceable back to at least one of these.

1

Clinician first, always

The portal is built for providers. Every default view, every hierarchy, every notification priority assumes the primary user is a clinician in a clinical environment, often mobile, often mid shift.

2

Institutional trust over visual novelty

This is not a consumer app. The visual language is navy heavy, structured, and authoritative. Patterns that feel "fun" in a consumer context feel unreliable in a clinical one.

3

Earn every word

Clinicians operate in high stakes, low attention environments. Every label, every error message, every empty state earns its place by being specific, direct, and actionable. No filler.

4

Replace, don't add

The portal exists because providers use too many disconnected tools. Every design decision should reduce the number of systems a clinician touches in a session, not introduce new ones.

5

Accessibility is clinical necessity

WCAG AA is the floor, not the target. Clinicians with color vision deficiencies, limited dexterity, or high glare environments need this portal to work. Accessibility is not optional in healthcare.

6

Motion earns focus, never demands it

Transitions support orientation and feedback. They never perform. Nothing in the portal bounces, spins unnecessarily, or competes with the content a provider came to act on.

Brand Foundation

What we build for.

The Relias Healthcare mission and four pillars are the root of every design decision in this portal. When a design question comes up without a clear answer, these answer it.

Mission Statement

"To cultivate environments where clinicians thrive and practice quality, reliable care, while providing our health system partners innovative solutions that achieve outcomes beyond what could be accomplished independently."

Tagline: Lean on us. No matter what. No matter when.
i. Priority

Clinicians are our Priority

Providers are the primary portal user. Every screen defaults to what a clinician needs first. Back office and admin functions are accessible but visually secondary.

ii. Partnership

Hospitals are our Partners

The portal reflects Relias as a reliable institutional partner. High trust visual treatment. Navy heavy, structured, no dark patterns.

iii. Purpose

Patients are our Purpose

This portal is internal, but the downstream impact is patient care. Design decisions should reflect the seriousness of that chain, clean, uncluttered, reliable under pressure.

iv. Passion

People are our Passion

Internal tools carry the same brand care as public facing ones. Labels, errors, and empty states reflect who Relias is, warm, direct, and clinician centric.

Brand Tone, Applied to Portal UI

Modern

Innovative, cutting edge, concise and technical language

Navigation labels, action buttons, and functional UI copy. Prefer the shorter, clearer word. "Save" not "Save Changes." "Add Shift" not "Create New Scheduling Event."

Authoritative

Knowledgeable, confident, and trustworthy

Error states, confirmations, system messages. State what happened. State what to do. "Shift overlap detected. Adjust your end time." No apologies.

Clinician Centric

Intentional, high integrity, unreasonably hospitable

Greetings, empty states, milestone moments. Name the provider. Be specific. "Good morning, Dr. Reynolds. Your next shift starts in 4 hours." Not "Welcome back."

Formal

Polished, sophisticated, reliable, and expert

Announcements, policy updates, documentation. Complete sentences. Sentence case throughout. Never all caps for body copy.

Logo System

Logo.

The Relias Healthcare "rh" mark, shown here in its four approved variants. Use the full color mark on light surfaces, the reversed white mark on navy and dark surfaces, and the single color teal or black marks where color is constrained.

rhReversed, on navy

Portal context

The portal header uses the white rh mark on the navy background at all times. The full enterprise lockup appears on the login screen and onboarding flow only.

Clear space & sizing

Minimum clear space equals the cap height of the mark on all sides. Minimum digital size: 24px tall for the mark, 96px wide for the full lockup. Never on busy or distracting backgrounds.

What not to do

No gradients on the mark. No drop shadows. No outline strokes. No color alterations. No rotating or stretching. No placing on an image without sufficient contrast.

Imagery

Imagery.

The portal inherits the Relias photography standard: authentic, professional, healthcare context imagery. No patient photography. Avatar and illustration use is tightly constrained.

Photography standard

Real life, professional images, expertise, kindness, competence. Bright and clear. Black and white acceptable but must stay bright, not dark or dramatic. No heavy filters.

What to avoid

No patient facing photography. No stock obvious poses. No imagery depicting distress, urgency, or negative clinical outcomes. This is a provider portal, not a patient facing product.

Provider avatars

Headshots: bright, professional, per Relias photography standards. Placeholder: initials on teal background. Circular crop always. 32px minimum in nav chip.

RHory mascot

Use sparingly in empty states, onboarding, and the AI assistant interface. Never in clinical or high stakes UI moments. Never in a way that animates distractingly.

Illustrations

Brand icon set for UI actions and navigation. Healthcare concept illustrations (clean lines, brand palette) for empty states and onboarding. Two color maximum. Illustrate to communicate, not to decorate.

Color overlays

When using photography behind UI (module headers, banners), apply teal or navy overlay using Multiply blending. Always use black and white base photo underneath to prevent color shifts.

Toast notifications

A toast is a small, self dismissing confirmation that slides in over the current screen, reports the result of an action, and fades after about five seconds. The dark surface floats above everything else in the interface.

Anatomy of a toast
1
Status icon signals the outcome at a glance, green success, blue info, amber in progress, red failure.
2
Title states what happened in three or four words.
3
Message gives the one detail the provider needs to confirm it worked.
4
Timing auto dismisses after five seconds, or stays until dismissed if it reports a failure.
When to use it

Use a toast to confirm a background result the provider does not need to act on, a shift saved, a trade request sent, a file uploaded. It reassures without interrupting the task in front of them.

Not for

Errors that require a decision, or anything the provider must read before continuing. Those belong in an inline alert or a modal, which stay on screen.

Menus, tooltips & popovers.

Three distinct patterns, easy to reach for the wrong one. A tooltip is a single line of text on hover/focus, never contains actions. A popover holds richer content, short copy, a small form, or a confirmation, and is triggered by a click, not a hover. A context menu is a dropdown menu triggered by right click (or a long press on touch), scoped to the specific item under the cursor.

Standards

Voice & copy.

Six portal copy examples across real UI contexts. Each reflects the Relias brand tone, Modern, Authoritative, Formal, Clinician Centric, applied to an internal provider tool. Use these as the tonal reference for any new portal copy.

Worked examples, real portal copy contexts
Homepage greeting

Good morning, Dr. Reynolds. Your next shift starts in 4 hours.

Time aware, specific, names the provider. No filler like "Welcome back!" The portal says what matters immediately.
Error, shift overlap

Shift overlap detected. You already have a confirmed shift from 7:00 AM to 7:00 PM on June 29. Adjust your end time or contact your scheduler.

No apology. States what happened, gives the specific conflict, tells the provider what to do. Active voice. Two sentences maximum for errors.
Empty state, no announcements

No announcements yet. New posts from your facility and Relias leadership will appear here.

An invitation, not a dead end. Tells the provider what they're looking at and what to expect without pitching the feature.
Shift confirmation toast

Shift confirmed: NMMC Emergency Dept., June 29, 7:00 PM to 7:00 AM. Check in at Gate 3.

All facts in order. No decoration. A provider reads this while walking into a facility, scannable in under three seconds.
Announcement, policy update

Updated documentation: Press Ganey distribution policy. Individual score reports will no longer be distributed per updated CMO guidance. See the full memo in Back Office.

States the change first, gives the reason, tells the provider where to go. Active voice. No corporate hedging.
RHory, first message

Hi Dr. Reynolds. Ask me anything, I can pull up policies, schedules, CME status, or help you navigate any module.

Warm but not performatively enthusiastic. Tells the provider specifically what it can do. The assistant leads with its capabilities, not generic helpfulness.

Standards

Motion.

Motion supports clinical focus, it never competes with it. Easings favor settle out curves. Durations stay short. Nothing bounces or overshoots. Reduced motion preferences are always respected.

Fast
--dur-fast · 140ms · ease-out
Hover states, focus rings, button press feedback. Immediate response, imperceptible delay between action and result.
Base
--dur-base · 220ms · ease-out
Card lifts, accordion expansions, tooltip reveals, dropdown menus. The default tempo for the entire portal.
Slow
--dur-slow · 360ms · ease-in-out
Modal entrance, page transitions, drawer slide. Reserved for moments that earn the time, not the default.
Run together to feel the tempo difference.
Easing curves
--ease-outcubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1)
--ease-iocubic-bezier(.65, 0, .35, 1)

No spring physics. No elastic overshoot. No bounce on hover or release. The portal does not perform.

Reduced motion

When prefers-reduced-motion: reduce is set, all transitions cap at 80ms or are removed entirely. No autoplay animation begins. Loading states use a static skeleton or spinner, not animated illustrations that could trigger vestibular issues.

Standards

Accessibility.

WCAG 2.1 AA compliance is required across the portal. A healthcare tool used in clinical environments needs to work for every provider. The items below are binding, not preferences.

  • ✓
    Color contrast
    AA minimum: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text and UI components. Relias Blue (#00bedd) is never used as a text or interactive element color, it fails at 2.23:1 on white. Provider Blue (#0378a6, 4.95:1) is the correct interactive color per the semantic token table in Section 02.
  • ✓
    Focus indication
    2px solid --focus-ring (#00bedd), offset 3px, on every interactive element. Teal provides sufficient contrast as a focus indicator on both light and dark surfaces (3:1 required for UI components, different threshold from text). Never set outline: none without a custom replacement already in place.
  • ✓
    Status without color alone
    Status badges always include a colored dot and a text label, never color alone. Error states include an icon and inline text in addition to a border color change. This is required for color blind users, which are not uncommon in clinical settings. Passing this check protects a meaningful percentage of your provider population.
  • ✓
    Touch targets
    44x44px minimum on all interactive elements, especially on mobile. All form inputs and buttons meet this requirement via the base component styles. Providers use this portal on mobile while on shift, touch accuracy matters and the interface will be used under pressure.
  • ✓
    Keyboard navigation
    Tab order follows visual reading order. Modal dialogs trap focus while open, focus must not escape to the background. Dropdown menus are fully keyboard accessible with arrow key navigation. All icon only buttons require aria-label attributes. Navigation sections use proper ARIA landmark roles: nav, main, aside.
  • ✓
    Form accessibility
    Labels are always visible, never placeholder only labels. Required fields are marked with both a visual indicator and aria-required. Errors are announced inline and via aria-live regions, not via color alone. Form groups use fieldset and legend where appropriate. Autocomplete attributes are applied to name, email, and date fields.
  • ✓
    Heading hierarchy
    One h1 per page. Headings nest in order without skipping levels, never jump from h2 to h4. Eyebrow labels styled as overlines do not replace a semantic heading element. Screen readers navigate by heading hierarchy, this must be correct in every portal view.
  • ✓
    Typography minimums
    Body text minimum 16px (--fs-base). Labels minimum 14px (--fs-sm). Metadata minimum 12px (--fs-xs). Line height minimum 1.5 for body, 1.1 for headings. Never reduce body text below 16px on mobile views.
  • ✓
    Reduced motion
    All transitions cap at 80ms or disable entirely when prefers-reduced-motion: reduce is set. No autoplay animation begins. This is a hard constraint, not a preference, vestibular disorders are a real clinical population and their needs must be respected in a healthcare tool.
  • ✓
    Screen reader behavior
    Dynamic content changes (toasts, alerts, live search results) use aria-live regions with appropriate politeness levels. Modal and drawer open states announce via role="dialog" and aria-labelledby. Tab changes announce the new panel via role="tabpanel". Loading states communicate via aria-busy.
  • ✓
    PHI handling in UI
    This portal handles protected health information. Session timeout UI must be clear, predictable, and give the user sufficient warning before expiry. PHI containing fields must not appear in URLs, browser history, error logs, or non authenticated views. Data masking and session handling implementation to be aligned with the HIPAA compliance framework.

Color blindness check

Simulated views of the core semantic palette under the three most common forms of color vision deficiency. Status is never conveyed by hue alone anywhere in the portal, icon and text label always accompany color, which is what keeps this palette usable under every simulation below.

Typical vision
Protanopia (red blind)
Deuteranopia (green blind, most common)
Tritanopia (blue blind, rare)

Under deuteranopia, success (green) and warning (amber) shift close together in hue, this is exactly why every status badge and alert pairs color with a distinct icon (✓ vs. ! vs. i) and a text label, never a color swatch alone.
