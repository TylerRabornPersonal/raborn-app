/ 01
Foundation

Foundation.
What Verdacta is.

Verdacta — from verdant and acta, the Latin word for official recorded acts — is the system of record for environmental compliance. Facilities upload their permits, plans, and agency correspondence; Verdacta turns them into a register of obligations, a calendar of deadlines, and a document vault, organized by company, site, and source.

Mission

To make every environmental obligation visible, dated, and provable — so nothing expires quietly and nothing is missed loudly.

01

On the record

Every requirement traces to a source — a permit, a page, a section. If Verdacta can't cite it, Verdacta doesn't claim it. The citation chip is the brand's handshake.

02

Deterministic where it counts

AI reads and organizes; code calculates and schedules. Due dates, limits, and calendars are computed, versioned, and reviewable — never improvised.

03

Calm under deadline

Compliance work is high-stakes; the interface never is. Quiet surfaces, one clear action, and status you can read across the room. Urgency is a color, not a siren.

/ 02
Voice & personality

Voice.
Plain, precise, accountable.

We sound like a senior environmental engineer writing a memo the agency might someday read: plain words, exact dates, no drama. Confident enough to be brief. Careful enough to cite.

We are

  • Exact — "DMR due March 28," never "coming up soon."
  • Sourced — claims carry citations: Permit 2280-00033 · p.14
  • Calm — overdue is a state to fix, not a panic to perform.
  • Field-literate — we say outfall, DMR, and Tier II without translating for the people who live them.

We are not

  • Legal counsel — we say "current" and "complete," never "you are in compliance" as a legal conclusion.
  • Startup-breezy — no exclamation points on deadlines, no emoji in the product.
  • Alarmist — no red walls of shame; one overdue item gets one honest flag.
  • Vague — "several requirements" is a failure; the number is in the database.
/ 03
Audiences

Four seats,
one register.

The same record serves everyone — what shifts is altitude. The EHS manager lives in tasks; the plant manager reads status; the director compares sites; the consultant signs off on what enters the record.

The EHS manager

Owns the calendar day to day. Needs tomorrow's inspections, this month's reports, and evidence uploads that take seconds. Verdacta is their working desk — lead with tasks and due dates.

The site / plant manager

Accountable but not hands-on. Needs a one-glance answer to "are we current, and what's at risk?" Lead with status, not features.

The corporate EHS director

Runs a portfolio — refineries, terminals, plants across states. Needs sites compared honestly: overdue counts, permit expirations, trend lines. Lead with the roll-up.

The consulting reviewer

The qualified engineer who approves what the AI extracted before it goes live. Needs source text beside the proposed requirement and a fast approve/edit/reject loop. Lead with traceability.

Logo system

Logo.
A wordmark & a stamp.

The wordmark sets "verdacta" in Spectral, lowercase — verd in Pine at medium weight, acta in Verdigris italic light. The seam between the two weights is the brand story: the green, and the record. For small spaces, the Va stamp — a square seal, copper-ruled, like an inspector's mark.

Usage proportion

The 65 / 20 / 10 / 5 rule

Sixty-five percent limestone and bone — the product should feel like paper on a drafting table. Twenty percent Pine for text, structure, and the app rail. Ten percent Verdigris for links, active states, and affirmative accents. Five percent for everything loud: status colors and copper details, earned, never decorative.

Typefaces

Typography.
A ledger hand & a civic hand.

Three families with strict jobs. Spectral — a bookish, slightly sharp serif — carries display and the wordmark: the voice of the record. Public Sans — the U.S. government's own open typeface — carries body and interface: the voice of the form that gets filed. Spline Sans Mono carries what must be exact: permit numbers, citations, dates, limits.

Spectral
Display serif

Headlines, section titles, pull quotes, and the wordmark. Light weights (200–300) at large sizes; the italic carries the brand's second voice. Never below 22px.

Type scale & spacing

A perfect fourth, on a 4px grid.

Core components

Components.
Paper surfaces, one clear action.

Bone cards on limestone pages, hairline sagegray borders, 8px radii, cool shadows. One primary action per view in Pine. Verdigris affirms; red is reserved for destructive and overdue.

Buttons

Pine is primary — one per view. Verdigris for secondary affirmative actions, ghost for tertiary. Red only for destructive or overdue-remediation actions. Focus is a 3px verdigris ring, never removed.

Forms, inputs & the dropzone

Labels above fields, sentence case, no placeholder-as-label. The upload dropzone is the product's front door — verdigris-tinted, generous, and explicit about what it accepts.

Extraction confidence is shown as a labeled meter — a percentage, a band, and what the band means for review routing. Empty states teach: what belongs here, and the one action that fills it.

Dashboard anatomy

Dashboards.
Status first, charts second.

A Verdacta dashboard answers three questions in order: is anything overdue, what's due next, and how does this period compare. Stat tiles carry the headline; charts carry the comparison; the register carries the detail. Values and labels always wear text tokens — color marks identity, never text.

Chart rules
  • One axis, always. Two measures of different scale become two charts, never a dual axis.
  • Fixed categorical order. Green, copper, river, plum — assigned to entities, never repainted when filters change.
  • Thin marks, 2px gaps, 4px rounded data-ends anchored to the baseline; grid and axes stay recessive sagegray.
  • Status colors never chart. An "overdue by month" series is drawn in a categorical hue with a labeled axis — red stays reserved for state.
  • Hover ships by default. Every mark gets a tooltip; every chart over one series gets a legend; every chart gets a table view.

/ 11
Iconography & imagery

Imagery.
The site as it is.

Icons are Lucide, 1.5px stroke, slategreen at rest and verdigris when active — never filled, never multicolor. Photography is documentary: real facilities in working light — tank farms at dawn, outfalls, gauges, gloved hands on valves. No drone-shot gloss, no stock handshakes, no leaves-and-dew "eco" clichés.

Infrastructure & steel
Water & treatment
Hands & instruments
Treatment

Neutral-to-cool white balance; let steel be gray and vegetation be green without saturation tricks. On dark heroes, a Pine 900 scrim at 45–60% keeps Bone text legible. Never duotone with Copper; never tint photography Verdigris.

Motion

Motion behaves like a file drawer: short, damped, certain. Fades and 8px rises at 160–240ms on ease-out. Status changes cross-fade; nothing bounces, pulses, or spins except the upload progress ring.

Accessibility

Accessibility.
Auditable means readable.

A compliance product that fails accessibility audits has no standing to exist. WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor; the pairings in section 05 are measured, and the rules below keep state and meaning legible to everyone.

a

Never color alone

Every status ships as chip + shape icon + words. The five state colors pass CVD simulation as a set, but the label is the guarantee, not the hue.

b

Focus is visible

3px Verdigris 400 ring, 2px offset, on every interactive element. Focus is restyled, never removed — this product is used with a keyboard on plant floors.

c

Fills are not text

Verdigris 500, Copper 500, and status base colors are fill colors. Running text uses their 700 steps or ink; amber text appears only at 18px+ bold.

@verdacta/ui

Tokens.
One source of truth.

The palette ships as CSS custom properties in the Raborn Digital monorepo as @verdacta/ui, consumed by Tailwind and shadcn. Components import the semantic layer; the raw scales stay an implementation detail.

Semantic CSS variables
/* packages/verdacta-ui/src/theme.css */ :root { /* brand */ --vd-primary: #2F5F4A; --vd-primary-deep: #1B382D; --vd-accent: #418D80; --vd-link: #285A50; --vd-detail: #A9683C; /* surfaces & ink */ --vd-surface: #FBFBF7; --vd-page: #F2F3EC; --vd-muted: #E4E7DB; --vd-border: #D3D7C8; --vd-ink: #161F1A; --vd-ink-muted: #737B6E; /* status — protected */ --vd-compliant: #3A7D34; --vd-due: #B87A1C; --vd-overdue: #B03A26; --vd-review: #3D74B8; --vd-superseded: #6E7468; }
shadcn mapping
/* shadcn semantic layer */ :root { --background: var(--vd-page); --foreground: var(--vd-ink); --card: var(--vd-surface); --primary: var(--vd-primary); --primary-foreground: var(--vd-surface); --accent: var(--vd-accent); --destructive: var(--vd-overdue); --ring: #64A89B; --muted: var(--vd-muted); --border: var(--vd-border); } // tailwind.config — extend.colors pine: { DEFAULT: '#2F5F4A', deep: '#1B382D' }, verdigris: '#418D80', copper: '#A9683C', limestone: '#F2F3EC', bone: '#FBFBF7' // fonts display: 'Spectral', sans: 'Public Sans', mono: 'Spline Sans Mono'

Full 100–900 scales (Pine, Verdigris) and the chip background/text pairs ship alongside as --vd-pine-100 … 900 etc. Chart tokens --chart-1 … 4 are fixed-order and validated; do not re-order or extend them without re-running the palette validator.
