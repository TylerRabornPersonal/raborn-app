The starting point

Foundation.
What AIMS exists to do.

Every visual and verbal decision that follows answers to this section. If a creative choice doesn't ladder up to the mission, voice, or audience defined here, it doesn't belong in the brand.

Mission

Adopted 2026

AIMS expands access to a values-aligned education by connecting generous taxpayers with the independent schools and families of the Mid-South, administering the federal tax-credit Scholarship Granting Program with discipline, transparency, and care.

Pillars

Three commitments that shape every decision.

i.

Stewardship

Every dollar a donor entrusts to AIMS is a promise. We administer those gifts with the discipline of a fiduciary and the warmth of a neighbor, and we report on them plainly.

ii.

Opportunity

We exist to put a values-aligned education within reach of the students who can most benefit from it. Access is the measure. Scholarships are the means.

iii.

Rootedness

We are of the Mid-South. We know the schools, the families, and the communities we serve, and we operate close to them, never as a distant administrator but as a present partner.

2.0
How AIMS sounds

Voice & personality.

AIMS sits at the intersection of a private foundation and a thoughtful neighbor. The voice has the gravity of a fiduciary and the warmth of a relative. Always principled. Never preachy. Always plainspoken. Never plain.

Principled, never preachy

We do
  • State what we believe in plain terms.
  • Trust the reader to weigh it.
  • Lead with what's true rather than what's flattering.
We don't
  • Lecture donors, schools, or families.
  • Lean on slogans or political shorthand.
  • Borrow other people's outrage.

Warm, never folksy

We do
  • Write like a person who knows the family by name.
  • Use concrete details over abstractions.
  • Allow a quiet sense of place to come through.
We don't
  • Affect a Southern accent on the page.
  • Use "y'all" or regionalisms in published work.
  • Mistake sentimentality for warmth.

Confident, never boastful

We do
  • Lead with results, attributed and verifiable.
  • Let the schools and students speak first.
  • Acknowledge what we don't yet know.
We don't
  • Use superlatives without evidence.
  • Compare ourselves to peers in print.
  • Mistake activity for impact.

Plainspoken, never plain

We do
  • Choose the shorter word when it's the truer word.
  • Allow a single, well-placed serif italic.
  • Write to a high schooler who's paying attention.
We don't
  • Use jargon, financial, educational, or political.
  • Dress thin ideas in expensive clothes.
  • Confuse formality with seriousness.
3.0
Who we serve

Four audiences, one brand.

AIMS speaks to four primary audiences. Visual identity stays cohesive across all of them; tone shifts subtly to meet each in their own register. Where decisions conflict, donor and family interests weigh equally.

First voice
Donors

Individuals and corporations claiming the federal scholarship tax credit. They want clarity, fiduciary confidence, and proof that their dollars reached a student. We give them numbers, names, and outcomes. Never platitudes.

First voice
Families

Parents and students choosing or already enrolled in a participating independent school. They want a clear path: who qualifies, what to submit, when funds arrive. We remove friction; we never patronize.

Operating partner
Schools

Member institutions of MAIS and partner schools across the four-state region. They want a reliable administrator that protects their independence and delivers funds on time. We are their back office, not their boss.

Governing body
Board & administrators

The AIMS Board of Directors and HMP staff who run the program day-to-day. They want internal tools and language that scale. Internal-facing materials follow the same brand standards as public ones.

Usage proportion

The 60 / 25 / 10 / 5 rule

Sixty percent navy and warm neutrals carry most surfaces. Twenty-five percent body text in slate and ink. Ten percent moss for accents, links, and emphasis. Five percent mulberry, reserved for moments of recognition, awards, certificates, donor honor rolls.

Subtle vertical shifts

One brand, four contexts

The same palette governs every audience. The proportional shift is the only thing that changes:

  • Donor: heavier navy and mulberry; financial-services register.
  • Family: heavier parchment and moss; warmer surfaces, more breathing room.
  • School: balanced navy and moss; tools-forward, less ornamental.
  • Internal: light navy on cream; functional, restrained, generous information density.

Where the brand lives.

Typography carries more of the brand than color does. Two families: a transitional serif used at light weights for display and emphasis, and a geometric humanist sans for body and interface. Used together, they read as both editorial and modern.

Measure

Body copy maxes at 70 characters per line. Long-form articles and editorial features may extend to 75ch. Below 45ch becomes choppy; above 80ch becomes hostile.

Italics in body

One italicized phrase per paragraph, maximum. Use for emphasis or named programs, never decoration. Body italics use the sans, not Petrona.

All caps

Reserved for eyebrows and overlines only. Always at 11 to 13px with 0.18em tracking. Never in body copy. Never in headlines.

Iconography

Icons.
Quiet & consistent.

Recommended icon set: Phosphor (Regular weight) for digital interfaces and custom hand-drawn glyphs for editorial moments. Stroke weight 1.5px at 24px size. Avoid mixing icon styles within the same surface.

Recommended set

Phosphor Icons (Regular), open-source, ~9000 icons, 6 weights. The Regular weight pairs cleanly with Figtree at our scale.

Sizing

16 / 20 / 24 / 32 / 48 px. Default to 20px in body text and 24px in navigation. Always align to optical center, not bounding box.

Color

Navy 800 default; moss 700 for active states. Reserve mulberry for award and recognition icons. Never multicolor.

10.0
Photography & illustration

Imagery.
Real, local, unposed.

AIMS imagery shows actual students, families, classrooms, and landscapes of the four-state region. Stock is the last resort, not the default. Every image should be capable of standing alone with a place name and a date attached.

Direction
  • Natural light, full color, high contrast. Avoid heavy color grading or filters. The South already has its own light.
  • Documentary over editorial. Real moments, not staged poses. Eye contact is fine but not required.
  • Wide context. Where possible, include the place, a classroom doorway, a hallway, a campus tree, a town square.
  • Hands and details. A signed application, a uniform crest, a textbook spine. Texture and specificity over abstraction.
  • Range of subjects. Reflect the actual demographics of the schools and families served, across race, class, geography, and grade level.
What to avoid
  • Stock photography of laughing diverse students at a desk. Generic. Honest local work always wins.
  • Antebellum architecture or imagery suggesting it. Even unintentional. Choose other regional anchors.
  • Confederate iconography of any kind. Including in incidental flags, statues, or backgrounds.
  • Heavy filters, vignetting, or duotone treatments that obscure the documentary character. The brand uses color filters only for tonal accents on graphics, never on faces.
  • Logo-stamped photos. Don't put the AIMS mark on a person's image. The image carries itself.

Motion & micro-interaction

Motion.
Quiet & intentional.

Motion supports the sense of an institution that takes its time and does its homework. Easings favor settle-out curves rather than springy bounces. Durations stay short. Reduce-motion preferences are fully respected.

Fast
--dur-fast · 160ms · ease-out
Hover states, focus rings, button press feedback. Imperceptible delay; immediate response.
Base
--dur-base · 240ms · ease-out
Card lifts, accordion expansions, tooltip reveals, dropdown menus. The default tempo.
Slow
--dur-slow · 400ms · ease-in-out
Modal entrance, page transitions, hero parallax. Reserved for moments that earn the time.
Run all three at once. The differences feel small in isolation but read clearly when stacked.
Easing curves
  • --ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)
  • --ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)

No spring physics. No elastic overshoot. AIMS does not bounce.

Reduced motion

When prefers-reduced-motion is set, all transitions cap at 80ms or are removed entirely. Hero parallax disables. Auto-playing animation never starts.

Voice in practice

Worked examples.
On-brand voice across contexts.

The same brand voice rendered in five common formats. Each example is on-brand. Each is also concrete enough to use as a tonal reference for new copy.

Donor appeal · email opener

Most scholarship organizations will tell you what they cost. We'd rather tell you what your gift does: which student, which school, which year. AIMS exists for that level of detail, and we'll keep finding ways to give it to you.

Confident without boasting. Concrete promise. Implicit critique of peers without naming them.
Student award letter · opening paragraph

Eleanor, congratulations. The scholarship committee at Jackson Preparatory School, working with AIMS, has selected you for a 2027 to 28 award. The decision was not casual; it was earned. What follows is what you can expect over the next few weeks.

Direct address. Recognizes the student's effort without being effusive. Sets up the practical content with quiet authority.
School partner email · subject line + first line

Subject: Disbursement schedule for FY2027, confirmed.

The first scholarship disbursement will arrive at your bursar's office on August 15. Below is the full schedule, the participating student roster, and the contact at AIMS who will own this for you all year.

Operational. No pleasantries. The schools are running schools, they want the information, not the relationship-building paragraph.
Press release · headline + lede

AIMS Scholarship Granting Organization Awards $30.6 Million in First Year, Reaching More Than 8,000 Mid-South Students.

JACKSON, Mississippi. AIMS, the federal tax-credit Scholarship Granting Organization serving independent schools in Mississippi, Louisiana, Arkansas, and Tennessee, today reported its first full year of awards.

AP-style headline. Lede answers who, what, where, when in one sentence. No adjectives.
Social post · LinkedIn / Facebook

Twenty thousand donors made this year possible. Eight thousand students started a school year because of them. We owe both groups a careful accounting, and we'll publish it next month, in plain numbers, with no rounding up.

Specific numbers without spin. The closing promise, "no rounding up", is the voice in one phrase.
Web hero · homepage

A scholarship is the difference between a school you'd choose and a school that's chosen for you. AIMS exists to keep that difference within reach.

Italics doing real work. Plainspoken, but the framing earns its keep. Works at any size, any device.

Accessibility

Accessibility.
A baseline, not a checkbox.

AIMS targets WCAG 2.2 AA at minimum, AAA where reasonable. Every component, color pairing, and motion behavior in this guide has been designed to that target. Treat the items below as binding.

  • ✓
    Color contrast
    AA minimum 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text. Approved pairings only, see Section 5.0. Never rely on color alone to convey state.
  • ✓
    Focus indication
    2px moss outline at 2px offset on every interactive element. Never remove the focus ring; never set outline: none without a custom replacement.
  • ✓
    Touch targets
    44 × 44 px minimum on mobile. Form inputs and buttons should never go below 44px tall. Stacked controls get 8px clearance minimum.
  • ✓
    Reading level
    Family- and student-facing copy targets a Flesch reading ease of 60+ (high-school accessible). Donor copy may sit between 50 to 60. Run every published draft through a reading-level check.
  • ✓
    Alt text
    Every image has alt text. Decorative-only graphics use alt="". Photos of students require descriptive alt that includes context, not just the subject.
  • ✓
    Forms
    Labels are visible, never placeholder-only. Required fields are marked. Errors are announced inline and via aria-live regions; not via color alone.
  • ✓
    Reduced motion
    All transitions cap at 80ms or disable entirely when prefers-reduced-motion: reduce is set. Auto-play does not begin under reduced motion.
  • ✓
    Heading hierarchy
    One h1 per page. Headings nest in order without skipping levels. Eyebrow text styled as overline does not replace a heading.
